01 / BodyAxis Y

A shell built to fold.

Most modern passenger cars are unibody: floor, pillars and roof form one welded structure instead of a body bolted to a separate frame. The cabin is a rigid safety cell. The front and rear are built to crumple, spending crash energy on bending metal instead of on the people inside.

02 / WheelsAxis Z

Four hand-sized patches.

Everything a car does, whether accelerating, braking or turning, passes through four patches of rubber, each about the size of your palm. Springs carry the weight, dampers stop the bouncing, and brake calipers squeeze discs to turn motion into heat.

03 / PowertrainAxis X

Fire, turn, deliver.

A petrol engine pushes pistons through four strokes: intake, compression, power, exhaust. The gearbox picks a ratio, the driveshaft carries torque rearward, and the differential lets the two driven wheels turn at different speeds in a corner.

Four strokes

Intake

The piston drops and draws in air and fuel.

Compression

The piston rises and squeezes the mixture.

Power

A spark ignites it and the expanding gas drives the piston down.

Exhaust

The piston rises again and pushes the burnt gas out.

Glossary

Unibody
A body and frame built as a single structure.
Crumple zone
A section designed to deform in a crash and absorb energy.
Contact patch
The part of a tyre touching the road at any moment, about the size of a palm.
Damper
Also called a shock absorber. It resists spring motion so the car settles instead of bouncing.
Differential
A gear set that lets the two wheels on one axle turn at different speeds.
Torque
A twisting force. The engine makes it, the driveline multiplies it, the wheels put it on the road.
